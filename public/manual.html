  • ⋯

Telling Sage things

This is the main thing you do. Say it however it comes out — Sage works out what it was.

  1. Tap the green Tell Sage circle.
  2. Type it, or tap 🎤 Talk and say it out loud.
  3. Tap Sort it out.
  4. Sage shows you what it thinks you meant. Tick the ones that are right, fix any wording, then save.

You can put several things in at once. "We're going to the lake Friday. I paid Terminix. Remind me to call about the water heater." — all three at the same time is fine.

Nothing is saved until you say so

Sage never files anything on its own. It proposes, you approve. If a suggestion is wrong, untick it and it's gone.

If Sage gets something wrong

Tap the item, and in the box that says "Say what's wrong with it" just tell it — "that isn't urgent, leave it until September." You don't have to hunt for the right field.

Now, Today and Week

Three views of the same life, at three distances.

🌿 Now

Built to fit one screen. The time and date, a greeting, today's weather, anything happening imminently, and your routine for this part of the day rolled up to a single line — tap it to open it in place.

First thing in the morning it shows a quieter screen with fireflies. It settles into the ordinary Now screen later on.

Under the greeting there's a line from Audrey. She can change it, or take it away, in Settings.

It asks for your weight once a day at home. At the lake it doesn't ask — the scale isn't there. You can still record one any time by telling Sage the number.

📋 Today

The full day: appointments from your calendar, what's due, what's overdue, and all your routines. If something is waiting on something else, Sage says so instead of nagging you about it.

🗓️ Week

The next seven days. Useful on a Sunday evening, or when someone asks if you're free Thursday.

Your own lists

A list is somewhere to put things. Nothing on one will ever chase you.

  1. ⋯ More → 🗒️ My lists
  2. Tap ＋ New list and give it a name — Costco, packing for the lake, things to ask David.
  3. Tap the list to open it, type into the box at the bottom, tap Add.
  4. Tap the circle beside anything to tick it off. It leaves the list but stays shown underneath for a while, in case you tapped the wrong one.
Lists stay out of your way on purpose

Paper towels on the Costco list will never appear on your Now or Today screen. It's a list, not a demand.

If you do want one to come forward on a particular day, open it and give it a date. Then it behaves like anything else.

Routines — making and changing them

A routine is a short list you work through. It comes back when its moment comes round, and stays out of the way the rest of the time.

⋯ More → 📋 Routines shows all of them, whether or not today is their day. They start folded to their names — tap a name to open that one, or Open all to see everything at once. You don't have to wait for a routine to come round to see what's on it.

Making a new one

  1. Tap ＋ New routine.
  2. Give it an icon and a name — "Opening the lake house", say.
  3. Choose when it comes round: every day, certain days of the week, certain months of the year, only when you're somewhere, before something happens, or whenever it suits.
  4. Add the steps, one per line. ＋ Add a step for another, ✕ to drop one.
  5. Tap Make the routine.
Opening and closing the lake

Pick Certain months of the year and tap the month it belongs in — April for opening, October for closing, or whichever suits. It will appear when that month comes and rest quietly until then.

Doing one when you feel like it

Some routines wait for their moment — Rain check appears when the forecast says rain, the lake list when a trip is close. If you want one today anyway, open it and tap Do this one today. It joins Today until midnight, and Take it off today puts it back to waiting.

You can also just tick its steps off right here on this screen, whether or not today is its day.

Changing one you already have

Tap Change this routine under any of them. Everything is editable — the name, when it comes round, the time of day, the steps. There's a delete button at the bottom if you'd rather it went away entirely.

"Skip it while I'm away" is worth knowing about: tick it and that routine won't ask anything of you from the lake. Nothing at home should be nagging you on holiday.

Subscriptions and things that come back

Your Subscriptions list from your phone is already in here, under Projects → Subscriptions & renewals.

All ten are there with their dates, and each one shows up in Coming up when it gets close. AARP, Norton, Frndly, the fishing licence, the roach spray, Dermalogica, Rollo's birthday, Paramount+, HBO Max and Terminix.

They come back round

These aren't jobs you finish once. When you tick one off, Sage moves it to its next date instead of closing it. Tick AARP in September and it's sitting there again next September.

To change how often something repeats — or to stop it — open the item and look for "Comes back round". Every week, every month, every 3 months, every year, or not at all.

Two things worth knowing

Your fishing licence expired on 4 July. It came across from your list that way. Worth sorting before the next trip to the lake.

Account numbers weren't copied over. The AARP membership number and the Terminix account number stayed in your Reminders app, where they already were. Sage doesn't keep numbers like that.

Your calendar, both directions

Two separate things, easy to mix up. Both live in ⚙️ Settings.

Your calendar → Sage

Sage reads your iCloud calendar. It never adds, changes or deletes anything there. Appointments show up in Today and Week, and a PT appointment automatically switches off your home PT rounds for that day.

If something looks stale, tap 🔄 Check for changes now at the top of that section.

Sage → your calendar

The other direction puts Sage's dates onto your real calendar, with proper alerts on your phone and watch. Subscribe once and new dates arrive on their own.

It sends appointments only. If you ever want your to-do items on there too, there's a switch for it — but your calendar stays a calendar by default.

About your Reminders lists

iCloud won't share your Reminders lists with any app outside Apple's own. That's Apple's decision, not a fault in Sage — which is why the two it does show have a ⚠️ in their names. Apple put that there.

So: your eleven lists stay in the Reminders app and work exactly as they always have. Anything you want Sage to know about, tell Sage directly, or keep it in My lists.

Changing your mind

Nothing in Sage is a one-way door.

Tick something by mistake? Tap it again. If it was a renewal that moved to next year, use undo and it goes back to the date it was on — you don't lose the year.

Anything Sage did on its own is listed with an undo button beside it. If you'd rather something simply weren't there, open it and tap Delete.

  • 🌙

A Custom GPT is a saved ChatGPT you add tools to; an ordinary conversation with Sage in ChatGPT isn't one. Making it takes a few minutes on a computer and needs a paid ChatGPT plan; the steps are in Settings.

You'll stay signed in — you shouldn't have to log in to your own morning.

What Sage will never keep

Decided at the start, and it hasn't moved.

No passwords, no security answers, no account or card numbers, no medical detail, no wills or estate documents. Sage will happily remind you to update the beneficiary — it just won't hold the account it belongs to.

Anything you'd rather the thinking part never saw: open the item and tick "Private from AI". Sage keeps it, and never sends it anywhere.

When something looks wrong

In order, cheapest first.

  1. If an appointment is missing: 🔄 Check for changes now in Settings.
  2. If a calendar or list looks empty, look underneath its name — Sage says what actually arrived, or what went wrong, in plain words.
  3. For iCloud specifically, What did Apple send? shows exactly what your account handed over. Tap Copy all of it and send that to Audrey.
  4. Anything else: tell Audrey what you were doing and what you expected instead. That's genuinely all that's needed.

Every problem found in this app so far was found by someone using it and saying "that looks wrong." That's not a nuisance — it's the only way these things get fixed.

What's been fixed so far → — everything you've reported and what changed because of it, so you never have to wonder whether you already asked.

Sage was built for Regena, to her own specification. It complements the things that already work rather than replacing them — your calendar stays your calendar, your Reminders stay your Reminders, and Sage holds the rest so it stops living in your head.
